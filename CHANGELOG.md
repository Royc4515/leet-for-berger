### Added (Web Push, stage F7 step 3, part B: the server)
- **The `push` Edge Function** (`deploy/supabase/functions/push`): `GET`/`POST /settings` (send hours, kinds off, time zone), `POST /subscribe` and `/unsubscribe` (one row per device; the same browser signing in as someone else moves to them), and `POST /run`, called at minute 0 of every hour by pg_cron with a secret header. At a user's send hour, in their time zone, it reads their synced record, picks the reminder with the same rules as the bell, logs it, and pushes it to every device they turned on (`jsr:@negrel/webpush@0.5.0`, a 4-hour time to live). A device the push service no longer knows (404 or 410) is dropped at once, one that fails 5 times in a row too. The logic is `core.js`, unit-tested in Node; the rules and problem titles are copied in by `scripts/content/build-push.mjs`, and `tests/push-server.test.js` fails when the copies are stale (run it after adding or retitling a problem).
- **No secret handled by a person**: the function makes its VAPID key pair on first use and keeps it in `push_keys` (service role only), serving the public half at `GET /key`; the hourly job's secret is generated inside Vault and read through `push_cron_secret()` (service role only). ADR 0011 item 9 says so.
- **Tables** `push_settings`, `push_subscriptions` and `push_log`, with row level security (`db/schema.sql`, `deploy/supabase/migrations/20261125_push.sql`): the log's key, one row per user, day and hour, keeps "at most one push per send hour" even if the job runs twice. Owner setup in `docs/DEPLOY_AUTH.md` section 8.

### Fixed (growth estimate on small-cache machines)
- **A cache step is no longer read as growth.** On a machine whose cache runs out between two input sizes, every larger size runs a constant factor slower, and honest linear or n log n solutions read "inconclusive" (three did on one CI runner, every time). The estimator now recognises one such jump, with the same rate before and after it and never on the last size, and takes it out before judging; quadratic code and growth that sets in stay as they were (ADR 0004 amendment, `cacheStep`, unit tests on the real CI timings). The same estimator draws the growth line in the browser, so readers on such machines get the honest verdict too.
